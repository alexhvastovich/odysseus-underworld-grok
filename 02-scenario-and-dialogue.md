# Scenario & Dialogue — Book 11 (Nekyia)

**Homeric source:** *Odyssey* Book 11, lines 90–151 (Teiresias's speech) and surrounding ritual frame.  
**Dramatic focus:** Odysseus performs the blood ritual; shades gather; Tiresias alone retains reason and delivers prophecy.  
**Omitted for 4:00 runtime:** Elpenor's full exchange, Anticleia reunion, heroin catalog — may be sequel sequence.

---

## Dramatic synopsis

Odysseus lands on the shore of Oceanus at the world's edge. He and his men carry offerings to the dank entrance of Hades. At a pit dug in the grey sand, he pours libations of milk, honey, wine, and water, then the blood of black sheep. The dead come — insubstantial, hungry, murmuring with eerie cries. **In the far darkness, legions of shades mass beyond the torchlight** — faces barely visible, pressing, starving for blood. Odysseus and his men **hold them back with drawn bronze swords** (threat, not battle — the dead are phantoms). Tiresias alone approaches with reason intact, staff in hand, blind but unwavering.

Tiresias tells Odysseus what the living gods have withheld: Poseidon's enduring wrath; the forbidden cattle of Helios; the suitors waiting in Ithaca; the bloody homecoming; and the strange inland journey with an oar on his shoulder until it is mistaken for a winnowing fan. Only then will his wanderings end in peace.

Odysseus listens. The weight of years not yet lived settles on his face. Behind them, the shades press at the edges of torchlight like smoke with faces.

## Emotional arc

1. **Dread** (Act I) — Odysseus chooses to descend; scale of the underworld
2. **Ritual tension** (Act II) — ceremony, dead stirring, control fraying
3. **Reverence** (Act III) — Tiresias is not an enemy but a judge
4. **Prophetic weight** (Act IV) — future collapses into present
5. **Acceptance** (Act V) — no answer satisfies; only forward motion

## Book 11: is there a fight?

**Short answer: No.** Homer does not stage combat in the Underworld.

What **does** happen:

1. **Sword standoff (Od. 11.48–50)** — After the blood is poured, souls flock to the pit "with faint gibbering." Odysseus **and his companions** hold them off with **drawn sharp swords** until Tiresias has drunk the blood and spoken. The dead are **phantoms** — you cannot fight them like men. It is intimidation and barrier, not a battle.

2. **Prophecy of future bloodshed** — Tiresias tells Odysseus he will return to Ithaca and **destroy the suitors** (that war happens in Books 21–22, not here).

3. **Failed embraces** — Odysseus tries to hug his mother's shade; arms pass through smoke. Tragic, not violent.

4. **Heroes' stories** — Agamemnon describes his murder; Achilles laments. Dialogue, not action.

**For this film:** Use **dread, scale, and drawn swords** — not a fight scene. The scariest dead should be **far away in the dark**, pressing inward.
